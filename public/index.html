What the research says (checked November 2026)

1. Reality check first

  • EU brokers must publish how many retail clients lose money on CFDs. ESMA's standard range is 74–89%; current broker disclosures are roughly 51–82%.
  • A Brazilian study of futures day traders who kept going 300+ days found 97% lost money (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti).
  • So: a high win rate means nothing on its own. 40% wins with 1:2 RRR makes money; 70% wins with 1:0.3 loses. For trades of a few minutes, the spread is the biggest enemy — this demo charges a realistic spread on every trade.

2. Strategies with real evidence (and what is in this app)

  • Opening Range Breakout on the Nasdaq — Zarattini & Aziz (2023) found strong results for a 5-minute ORB on QQQ (2016–2023), but their test assumed no spread or slippage; an independent replication on index CFDs found results near zero after costs. → NY ORB (NAS100, gold).
  • Intraday momentum — Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (Journal of Financial Economics, 2018): the market's first half hour predicts its last half hour; a simple timing rule earned ~6–7% a year on the S&P 500 ETF. → Intraday Momentum (NAS100, 15:30–16:00 New York).
  • London breakout — popular, but the high win rates (58–65%) quoted online come from vendors with no visible method; one transparent 10-year EUR/USD test lost money (36% wins, profit factor 0.87). → London BO (EUR/USD, gold), only traded if it passes the test.
  • Intraday momentum with a "noise area" — Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon (2024) trade only when price leaves its normal intraday range around the open, and found it worked on the S&P 500 ETF. Works all day. → Noise Mom. (all 3 markets).
  • Liquidity sweep reversal and volatility squeeze breakout — price-structure ideas used by many discretionary traders (stop hunts beyond yesterday's high/low; quiet markets breaking out). No strong academic proof, so they must earn their place in the backtest.
  • Trend pullback and Bollinger mean reversion — classic textbook rules without strong academic proof. Included so they can be measured, not because they are proven.

News filter: big scheduled news (US inflation, jobs report, Fed decisions, GDP…) makes prices jump in seconds, stops get skipped, and spreads widen. The app takes no new trades from 30 minutes before to 15 minutes after high-impact US news (plus euro news for EUR/USD), using the free Forex Factory calendar.

Time rule: trades run until TP or SL; whatever is still open at 16:55 New York (end of the trading day) is closed. Removing the old 60-minute limit changed the results a lot — the London breakout went from losing to the best strategy, because many of its winners needed a few hours.

3. How the app chooses

Every strategy × market is backtested on the last 2–3 months of 5-minute data (what Yahoo provides) with spreads. Every chart is handled on its own: each strategy is tried in several settings versions on that chart, the best version is chosen on the first two-thirds of the history (training), and it must then also make money on the last third, which it was never tuned on (unseen test), with at least 15 trades in total. In Auto mode each chart trades only its own best passing strategies (up to 2), with the settings that worked on that chart. Results are re-checked every time you open the app. Backtests are always more optimistic than live trading — the live demo is the real test.

4. Copying "the best traders"

  • Legal? Yes in the EU, when done through a regulated (MiFID) firm. ESMA treats copy trading as portfolio management or investment advice (Supervisory Briefing on copy trading, 2023), so the platform needs the right licence. Always check the firm on the regulator's register (e.g. ASF in Romania, CySEC, FCA).
  • Where: eToro CopyTrader (its public API added investor rankings and copy endpoints in 2026), MetaTrader 4/5 Signals (MQL5), cTrader Copy (via brokers such as Pepperstone or IC Markets), ZuluTrade, Darwinex (FCA/CNMV, risk-scored "DARWINs").
  • Careful: leaderboards show the survivors. Traders who blow up close the account and disappear; leaders often profit more than the people copying them (lag, spread, different account size). Martingale/grid systems show a beautiful smooth curve right until they crash.
  • How to vet a trader: at least 12 months of real-money history, max drawdown under ~20–25%, trade size that does not grow after losses (no martingale), sensible leverage, and copiers who stay for months.
  • I did not pick specific people to copy: I'm not a licensed financial advisor, and a leaderboard rank alone is not a good reason to trust someone. The demo can't copy them anyway without an account and API access on that platform.

5. Market data used

Free Yahoo Finance data. EUR/USD is spot FX. Gold uses COMEX gold futures (GC=F) and NAS100 uses E-mini Nasdaq-100 futures (NQ=F) as stand-ins — they move almost the same as broker CFD prices but the price level differs a little, and exchange data may be slightly delayed. That's fine for a demo because entries and exits are simulated on the same feed.
